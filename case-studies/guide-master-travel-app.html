Framing

  1. ProblemVisiting a city you don't know is hard: travellers struggle to find the best spots and a guide whose personality fits theirs.
  2. What I didA 15-question survey (30 responses), a benchmark of BlaBlaCar, Fiverr and Yoojo, persona, empathy and journey maps, then zoning, design system, UI and prototype.
  3. ImpactA tested registration and booking journey; findings from 5 user tests fixed in the next iteration.

01 Context

Context and question

Guide Master is a fictional matchmaking service that pairs tourists with a local guide based on personality. The project focused on the registration and booking journey for travellers looking for an authentic, personal experience.

How might we help travellers find a guide they trust and book them easily?

02 Method

Approach and methodology

  • 15survey questions
  • 30responses
  • 3apps benchmarked
  • 5user tests

We benchmarked three apps that connect individuals and professionals for services, BlaBlaCar, Fiverr and Yoojo, because their journeys resemble ours. This helped us define the Guide Master user journey.

03 Data

Synthesis

81.8% of respondents described themselves as organisers rather than followers. Travel habits shifted with age, from family trips at 18–24 to couple trips at 25–34 and trips with friends later on.

04 Key insights

Key insights

  1. I-01

    Insight 1: Travellers want a guide who knows the place

    Knowledge of the place (33.9%) and advice (26.1%) were the top expectations.

    Evidence
    Survey · n = 30
    Decision
    Show local knowledge and reviews first on guide profiles
    Result
    Profiles with ratings, motto and activity packages
  2. I-02

    Insight 2: Price and the right match are the main barriers

    Price (39.8%) and finding the good match (26.1%) were the top pain points.

    Evidence
    Survey · n = 30
    Decision
    Personality-based matching and clear package prices
    Result
    A matching flow and transparent packages

05 Tests

User tests and iteration

We built the full mockup and prototype in 2 days on a shared design system, then ran 5 user tests with people matching our personas.

What worked

  • Smooth, simple navigation and a well understood rating system.
  • An intuitive payment process with several options.
  • Clear confirmation for cancelled bookings and the ability to go back.

What we fixed next

  • The explorer / guide pop-up was too cluttered.
  • An explanatory pop-up was missing after registration.
  • The home visual made the "Match" call to action confusing, and could be misread by couples.
  • "See more" and "See profile" looked like the same action.
  • Plus and minus were inverted in the participant picker; users wanted more criteria for the guide's personality.

06 Retrospective

What I learned and what I would do differently

  • The 5 user tests caught problems we had missed, such as the confusing Match button and two links that looked like the same action.
  • The home visual looked good but made the Match button confusing: a visual has to support the action, or it gets in the way.
